### Engagement band (16.59 m)

The band is the AASHTO stopping-sight-distance form d = v·t_r + v²/(2a), but the parameters are changed:

| parameter set | t_r | a | d at 25 km/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| MARSHAL | 1.0 s | 2.5 m/s² | 16.59 m |
| AASHTO | 2.5 s | 3.4 m/s² | 24.45 m |

- The code calls MARSHAL's parameters "literature-range candidates". No source supports t_r = 1.0 s. The only source
  named locally gives 1.5–2.5 s, and it is outside the repository and marked unverified.
- **Classification:** mathematically adapted, with parameters of unresolved provenance
  ([literature_grounding.md](literature_grounding.md)).

### Oracle-only margins

These are controller targets, not scoring thresholds:

- 2.5 m stop margin;
- 1.3 m pull-up when the spawn is inside 4.0 m;
- 3.5 / 6.0 m/s² envelopes.

They must not be read as properties of the score.

### Reading

- **Verdicts and the ranking are robust to every perturbed threshold.** Kendall τ-b is ≥ 0.91 everywhere. Most of the
  recorded behaviour is far from every threshold (see [score_distribution.md](score_distribution.md) §5).
- **The engagement band has no effect at any scale.** The recorded episodes never fall inside the ±25% range around
  16.59 m. This matches the scenario contracts: every one of the 14 STOP/HOLD spawns (all except
  `dual_authority_handoff`) is already inside the band, through the stop line or the officer at about 13.4 m. The
  16.59 m derivation therefore carries no measurement weight in v2.
- **Robustness to thresholds is not validity.** The problems found (F1–F4) are structural, and no single threshold
  change removes them. Tuning thresholds against the leaderboard was not done, and should not be.
- **Scope.** There is one deterministic run per stack, so these figures describe scorer sensitivity, not run-to-run
  stability. Five VLM runs show seed spread (SD 0.25–3.41 graded); they do not establish broad statistical
  certainty.
