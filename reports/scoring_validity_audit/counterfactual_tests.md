  [`counterfactual_raw.md`](counterfactual_raw.md), generated by `scripts/counterfactual.py`.
- **Machine-readable results:** `data/counterfactual_results.json`.

## Method and its limits

**How the synthetic telemetry is built.** Each case starts from a recorded oracle episode (seed 0):

- `green_stop` for STOP cases;
- `red_proceed` for PROCEED cases;
- the respective scenario for the 25-scenario policy sweep.

That episode supplies every non-kinematic column: officer phase and gesture, light, onset and duration. A scripted
speed profile then sets the ego speed, and the route position is its integral. The stop-line and officer distances
are derived from the spawn geometry. The junction flag follows the template's first junction row, and collision
events are injected where a case requires them.

**What this tests:** the logic of the scorer, using the pinned v2 strict and graded functions with `protocol="v2"`.

**What it does not test:**

- whether a CARLA vehicle can follow the profile;
- lateral motion (it is held at 0);
- actor reactions.

A real-data cross-check exists for the main finding. The oracle passes strict STOP cells after only 1.23–2.67 m of
travel (`occluded_officer`, `sequential_directive`, `fallen_person`; NEAT `unauthorized_go` 1.70 m). So short
approach-then-stop trajectories occur in CARLA and pass. A scripted lurch controller has **not** been run in CARLA.

### #14 in detail

- **What the lurch policy passes (strict).** It passes 13 STOP/HOLD scenarios in which the spawn is already inside
  the 16.59 m band. In 11 of them the directive is live from 1.0 s; `fallen_person` has onset 0 s, and
  `flagger_slow_then_stop` enforces its final STOP from 6.05 s (the lurch is already stopped by then):
  - `green_stop`, `signal_off`, `fallen_person`, `unauthorized_go`, `adjacent_lane`, `flagger_control`;
  - `conflicting_authorities`, `sequential_directive`, `ambiguous_gesture`, `flagger_slow_then_stop`;
  - `two_civilians_disagree`, `fake_vest_director`, `school_crossing_guard`.

  The shorter 0.72 s variant (≈1.2 m) also passes `occluded_officer` (14/25, 62.4). There the spawn is only 2.5 m
  from the line, so 1.6 m of travel breaks the 1.0 m margin.
- **What it fails.** It fails every move-required cell and `dual_authority_handoff`, which has an approach
  requirement.
- **Independent confirmation.** A separate build of the same counterfactual (`scripts/blip.py`: 1.17 m, peak
  7 km/h) gives strict PASS and credit 1.0 on all 14 STOP/HOLD scenarios other than `dual_authority_handoff`
  ([scenario_contracts.csv](scenario_contracts.csv)).

## Summary of verdicts

**Programming defects (implementation ≠ stated intent):**

- **F3: collision-event counting.** The docstring and README describe a collision **count**. What is counted is
  sensor callbacks.

**Specification-level properties that produce questionable orderings.** These are not programming bugs: the code
does what the specification says.

- **F2:** a movement-eligibility gate that a 1–2 m lurch defeats. Together with every STOP spawn sitting inside the
  engagement band, this lets an instruction-independent policy score 59.8.
- **F4:** the anti-creep design rewards a faster approach at equal overshoot.
- **#6:** a 5 km/h jump discontinuity.
- **#13:** non-identifiable PROCEED geometry.

**A specification defect introduced by v2 (an interaction):**

- **F1: onset-relative approach window.** The v1-era engagement rule assumed a moving approach at onset. v2 starts
  every system at rest one second before onset, which breaks that assumption.
